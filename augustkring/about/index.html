My story.

I grew up in an entrepreneurial family with roots in e-business and venture building. While I was still at school, I spent time with friends developing ideas for online services and subscription businesses. Most never made it beyond the concept, but I enjoyed thinking through the whole business: who it was for, why people would want it and how it could work in practice.

At 18, I left school after being offered the chance to run a VR venue in Copenhagen, where I had been working part-time. I became responsible for a small team and nearly every side of the business, from finances and staffing to customer experience and daily operations. Later, I also helped develop new services and ventures, including VR rental, events and a customer community. I enjoyed the freedom to create new things, but also learned what it takes to make them work day to day.

Over time, I took on more than I could sustain. I burnt out and spent two years away from work. When I returned, I took on operational responsibility again, this time in a professional kitchen. Those experiences changed how I think about ambition and responsibility. A business is more than its ideas and plans. The people, decisions and everyday demands matter just as much, especially if you want to build something that lasts.

As generative AI became more capable, I grew interested in a bigger question than how to make individual tasks faster: what changes when we rethink how the company itself works? That interest led me to KRING, where I worked on AI-native products and built software using autonomous development agents. It brought together several parts of my experience, from business strategy and product development to operations and the practical use of new technology.

Today, I'm building my own products and businesses with that broader perspective. I'm interested in how companies can make better use of their knowledge, people and technology, and keep building on what they learn. The technology has changed, but many of the questions I care about remain the same: is the problem worth solving, does the business make sense, and can we build something that creates lasting value?

Principles.

Think big. Start small. Scale fast.

Aim high, but start small enough to test the idea. When it works, there's a reason to move faster.

Execution over fear.

Move before everything is certain. Take the next useful step, learn from the result and adjust.

Learn, unlearn, relearn.

Stay curious, question what you know and be willing to change your mind when the evidence changes.

People, planet & profit.

Build for commercial success without overlooking people or the planet. All three shape how the business works.

Different lenses.

The Big Five report describes August as highly open to new ideas and very extraverted, with high Conscientiousness and exceptionally low Agreeableness. His particularly high Orderliness score adds a preference for structure to an otherwise exploratory, outgoing profile.

The report associates this combination with curiosity, assertiveness and independent judgement. It also points to a direct, sceptical style that can be experienced as blunt or confrontational, especially when patience, tact or compromise are needed.

HIGH5 ranks August's leading strengths as Strategist, Coach, Catalyst, Philomath and Commander. Its descriptions portray someone who sees the bigger picture, notices potential in others, gets things moving, enjoys learning and is comfortable taking a clear position.

The same strengths have their rough edges. The report notes impatience with slow decisions or stalled progress, frustration when people's potential goes unused, and a willingness to confront disagreement that may feel too direct to others.

VIA places Creativity, Perspective, Appreciation of Beauty & Excellence, Spirituality and Love of Learning at the top of August's character strengths. In this framework, they describe originality, an ability to see the bigger picture, attention to quality, interest in meaning and enjoyment of learning.

Together, these strengths portray someone who is drawn not only to new ideas but also to understanding their wider significance. Perspective concerns seeing situations from more than one angle, while Appreciation of Beauty & Excellence includes noticing skill and quality in many forms.

BG5 identifies August as an Express Builder, a career type that its system associates with sustained energy for engaging work and moving quickly from inspiration to action. His interaction style is described as Respond, then Engage, rather than acting on every impulse to initiate.

The report calls his decision-making approach Respond then Wait for Clarity, giving emotional decisions time to settle. It labels his way of processing information Collaborative, helped by movement and changing surroundings, and treats satisfaction and frustration as signs of how well work fits.

August's MBTI type is listed as ENFP. In that framework, ENFPs are described as sociable, imaginative and alert to possibilities, often finding connections between ideas and drawing energy from conversations and new experiences.

The ENFP profile also favours flexibility and personal values over rigid routines. Alongside enthusiasm and an ability to improvise, it describes potential difficulties with repetitive detail, too many competing interests and following through once the initial excitement has faded.

The Enneagram type listed for August is 7w8, or Type Seven with an Eight wing. In this framework, Seven is associated with variety, curiosity and a desire for freedom, while the Eight wing brings a more assertive, independent and action-oriented style.

This combination describes someone drawn to fresh opportunities and reluctant to feel constrained. Its potential difficulties include impatience, taking on too much and moving quickly towards the next possibility rather than staying with discomfort or unfinished work.

August's Human Design report identifies him as a Manifesting Generator with a Wait to Respond strategy, Emotional (Solar Plexus) Authority and a 2/4 Hermit/Opportunist profile. Within this system, the type is associated with energy for engaging work and moving quickly once something prompts a response.

The report also describes a tendency to skip steps when moving too fast and says important decisions require time for emotional clarity. Its 2/4 profile combines a wish for solitude with opportunities arising through other people, while satisfaction and frustration are presented as its main signals.
