About.

I grew up in an entrepreneurial family with roots in e-business and venture building. Business was therefore something concrete to me from an early age: a way of turning ideas into things that could actually exist. By my early teens, I was already trying to build small projects with friends and learning mostly by doing.

At 18, after a couple of years working part-time at a VR venue in Copenhagen, I was offered the chance to run it. I left school and took the job. The team was small, so I became involved in almost every part of the business and gradually moved further into business development, new commercial initiatives and spinouts.

I also learned the limits of that pace. I kept taking on more and pushing harder until I burnt out. Recovery took time, but it changed the way I think about performance, leadership and the kind of companies I want to help build. Ambition still matters to me. So do judgement, sustainability and the human side of the work.

When generative AI began to accelerate, I became fascinated by what it could change about how companies are built and run. That interest led me to KRING, where I worked on AI-native products and agentic AI alongside people with deep experience in the field.

Today, I am building again. I am still drawn to ambitious ideas, new technology and early-stage uncertainty, but I care much more about turning that energy into companies that are useful, resilient and worth building.

Skill Set

The capabilities I use most often when turning a new idea into a real product or business.

Agentic AI & Strategy

Identifying where AI can create meaningful leverage, then translating that opportunity into practical systems, agents and workflows.

Strategic Innovation

Connecting changes in technology, markets and customer behaviour to new opportunities, then deciding which ones are worth pursuing.

Business Development

Shaping business models, partnerships and commercial opportunities around an idea so it has a realistic path to growth.

Product Development

Turning an idea into a focused product, learning from real use and using that evidence to decide what to build next.

Market Validation & GTM

Testing the riskiest assumptions early, then turning what we learn into clearer positioning, demand and a practical route to market.

Mindset

The principles that shape how I make decisions, work with others and move from idea to execution.

Think big. Start small. Scale fast.

Set an ambitious direction, start with the smallest useful test and scale when the evidence shows that the idea deserves it.

Execution over fear.

Move before everything is certain. Take the next useful step, learn from reality and adjust without losing momentum.

Learn, unlearn, relearn.

Stay curious, question your own assumptions and change your mind when better evidence or experience points somewhere else.

People, planet & profit.

Build for economic value, human value and wider impact at the same time, rather than treating one as the cost of another.

Human-centric by design.

Use technology to strengthen human capability and judgement, while keeping the people affected by it at the centre of the work.

Different lenses.

The clearest pattern in my Big Five results is a combination of curiosity, energy, structure and directness. I am strongly drawn to new ideas, complex problems, people and learning, but I also prefer clear standards and enough order to turn ideas into something concrete.

The same profile suggests I am comfortable challenging assumptions and saying what I think. Under pressure, that can become impatience or sharpness, so one of the useful counterweights for me is slowing down enough to keep directness constructive.

My HIGH5 results point to a similar working pattern. Strategist and Philomath reflect how naturally I look for patterns, learn broadly and connect ideas. Catalyst and Commander fit my tendency to create momentum and speak directly, while Coach reflects the part of me that enjoys helping capable people grow.

Taken together, the profile also explains some of the friction points: slow decisions, wasted potential, unnecessary waiting and indirect communication can frustrate me. The useful lesson is to pair speed and clarity with enough patience for other people and the context around them.

VIA places Creativity, Perspective, Appreciation of Beauty & Excellence, Spirituality and Love of Learning at the top of my profile. Together, they describe a strong pull towards new ways of thinking, making sense of complexity, noticing quality, looking for meaning and continuously expanding what I know.

That combination brings original thinking and wider perspective together. Learning is not just about collecting information, and creativity is not only about making something new; both are ways of understanding what matters and seeing more possibilities in the work.

My BG5 report describes me as an Express Builder. Its core recommendation is to respond to what is actually in front of me before engaging, then bring sustained creative energy to work that feels right. Satisfaction is described as the positive signpost, while frustration is a signal that something is off.

The report adds an important decision-making constraint: larger commitments should wait for emotional clarity rather than be made at the first high or low. It also describes my assimilation style as collaborative, with movement and interaction helping information settle.

My MBTI result is ENFP. In the Myers-Briggs framework, ENFPs are associated with enthusiasm, imagination and a strong orientation towards possibilities. Their dominant process, Extraverted Intuition, is described as noticing patterns and connections in the external world and sharing them with others.

That makes the framework useful mainly as a description of how I tend to explore: broadly, socially and with an interest in what could be. A strong pull towards novelty and possibility also makes it important to know when exploration has done its job and a direction needs to be chosen.

My Enneagram result is 7w8. The Enneagram Institute describes Type 7 as optimistic, versatile and variety-seeking, with a tendency towards overextension, impatience and impulsiveness. The Eight wing adds a more assertive and self-directed edge.

The useful tension in that description is between breadth and depth. A profile that is constantly pulled towards new possibilities benefits from choosing what deserves sustained attention rather than simply what is most stimulating next.

My Human Design report identifies me as a Manifesting Generator with a strategy to Wait to Respond, Emotional Solar Plexus Authority and a 2/4 Hermit/Opportunist profile. Within the framework, Manifesting Generators are described as capable of moving quickly from response into action, with a corresponding risk of skipping steps when they move too fast.

Emotional Authority adds the recommendation to give larger decisions time to reach clarity. The 2/4 profile describes a tension between developing natural abilities in private and being called into opportunities through relationships and networks. Those are the parts of the framework I find most useful as reflection.
