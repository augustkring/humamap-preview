Hi, I’m August.

I believe the future is shaped by what we choose to build together.

My story.

I grew up in an entrepreneurial family with roots in e-business and venture building. While I was still at school, I spent time with friends developing ideas for online services and subscription businesses. Most never made it beyond the concept, but I enjoyed thinking through the whole business: who it was for, why people would want it and how it could work in practice.

At 18, I left school after being offered the chance to run a VR venue in Copenhagen, where I had been working part-time. I became responsible for a small team and nearly every side of the business, from finances and staffing to customer experience and daily operations. Later, I also helped develop new services and ventures, including VR rental, events and a customer community. I enjoyed the freedom to create new things, but also learned what it takes to make them work day to day.

Over time, I took on more than I could sustain. I burnt out and spent two years away from work. When I returned, I took on operational responsibility again, this time in a professional kitchen. Those experiences changed how I think about ambition and responsibility. A business is more than its ideas and plans. The people, decisions and everyday demands matter just as much, especially if you want to build something that lasts.

As generative AI became more capable, I grew interested in a bigger question than how to make individual tasks faster: what changes when we rethink how the company itself works? That interest led me to KRING, where I worked on AI-native products and built software using autonomous development agents. It brought together several parts of my experience, from business strategy and product development to operations and the practical use of new technology.

Today, I'm building my own products and businesses with that broader perspective. I'm interested in how companies can make better use of their knowledge, people and technology, and keep building on what they learn. The technology has changed, but many of the questions I care about remain the same: is the problem worth solving, does the business make sense, and can we build something that creates lasting value?

Product Development

Turning an idea into a focused product, learning from real use and using that evidence to decide what to build next.

Market Validation & GTM

Testing the riskiest assumptions early, then turning what we learn into clearer positioning, demand and a practical route to market.

Mindset

Entrepreneurial

I look for opportunities, move ideas into action and prefer learning through building rather than waiting for perfect certainty.

Strategic

I look for patterns, connect the dots and focus attention on the few choices that can materially change the direction.

Curious

I am drawn to new ideas, unfamiliar problems and learning that changes how I understand the world and what might be possible.

Adaptive

I respond to new information, change course when the evidence changes and stay comfortable while the answer is still forming.

Connective

I connect ideas, people and perspectives, and I often do my best thinking through dialogue, movement and collaboration.

Principles.

Think big. Start small. Scale fast.

Aim high, but test the idea before betting everything on it. Start small, learn what works and scale when the evidence is there.

Execution over fear.

Uncertainty is unavoidable. Take the next responsible step, face the result and keep learning instead of waiting for perfect confidence.

Learn, unlearn, relearn.

What once worked may no longer be right. Keep learning, question your assumptions and be willing to change your mind.

People, planet & profit.

Build for commercial success without treating people or the planet as an afterthought. All three have to shape how the business works.

Different lenses.

My Big Five results show a striking mix: very high openness and extraversion, a strong preference for order, and very low agreeableness. In everyday terms, that fits someone who enjoys unfamiliar ideas and people, thinks independently, and is comfortable questioning the way things are done.

The combination also explains a tension I recognise. I like working with others, but I'm not particularly interested in agreement for its own sake. Directness can bring clarity, but it can also become impatience or bluntness if I don't give people enough room.

My five HIGH5 strengths are Strategist, Coach, Catalyst, Philomath and Commander. They bring together pattern recognition, learning, helping others develop, creating momentum and being willing to take a clear position. I recognise that combination when a new idea needs both direction and people who can move it forward.

The risk is moving faster than the people around me. Something that already feels clear to me may still need discussion for someone else. Momentum is more valuable when other people understand and own the direction too.

My VIA profile puts Creativity, Perspective, Appreciation of Beauty & Excellence, Spirituality and Love of Learning at the top. It describes a different side of the same picture: a strong interest in ideas, quality, learning and questions of purpose, not only in getting things done.

I care about whether something is thoughtfully made and whether it is worth making at all. That matters to me in business and technology as much as in design. A commercially promising idea is not automatically a good one.

BG5 describes my career type as Express Builder, with Respond then Engage, emotionally clear decisions and satisfaction or frustration as signs of whether the work fits. It also describes my information-processing style as Collaborative.

The part I recognise is the difference between having energy and knowing where to direct it. I can move quickly when something engages me, but complex information often needs time to settle. Conversation, movement and changing surroundings can help me make sense of it.

My MBTI result is ENFP, a type associated with exploring possibilities, noticing connections and developing ideas through interaction. That reflects the part of me that enjoys open questions and conversations that lead somewhere unexpected.

It doesn't capture everything. I'm also direct, opinionated and keen to make decisions, which is easy to miss in a broad type description. For me, the useful distinction is knowing when to keep exploring and when it's time to choose a direction.

My Enneagram result is 7w8. In that framework, Type Seven is drawn to variety, possibilities and freedom, while the Eight wing adds independence and assertiveness. I recognise the attraction of a new opportunity and the energy that comes with wanting to make something happen.

The risk is mistaking movement for progress. When there are many interesting directions, choosing one means leaving others aside. This description is most useful to me as a reminder that depth and follow-through matter just as much as enthusiasm.

My Human Design report describes me as a Manifesting Generator, with a Wait to Respond strategy, Solar Plexus Authority and a 2/4 Hermit/Opportunist profile. Where BG5 focuses on work, Human Design places related ideas in a broader personal context, including decisions, relationships and the balance between independence and connection.

I treat it as a framework for reflection rather than a scientific personality measure. What resonates is the tension between working deeply on my own and finding opportunities through other people. Being ready to act isn't always the same as being ready to commit.
