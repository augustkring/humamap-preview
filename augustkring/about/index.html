Hi, I’m August.

I’ve spent most of my working life close to new ideas, early-stage businesses and the messy work of turning possibilities into something real. What keeps me interested is how people, business and technology can come together to create useful, positive change.

How I got here.

I grew up in an entrepreneurial family with roots in e-business and venture building, so business never felt especially abstract. By my early teens, I was already trying to build things with friends, mostly because I liked the feeling of taking an idea and making it real.

At 18, after a couple of years working part-time at a VR venue in Copenhagen, I was given the opportunity to run it. I left school and took the job. In a small team, roles were fluid, so I learned by being close to almost every part of the business. Over time, I gravitated towards business development, new commercial initiatives and spinouts.

I also learned what happens when ambition is not matched by limits. I kept taking on more and pushing harder until I burnt out. Recovery took time, but it forced me to rethink how I wanted to work, lead and live. It made the human side of business much harder to ignore and changed what I considered sustainable performance.

When generative AI began to take off, I became fascinated by what it could change about how companies are built and run. That interest eventually led me to KRING, where I worked on AI-native products and agentic AI with people who had been deep in the field for years.

Today, I’m building ventures again. I still like speed, ambition and new possibilities, but I care more about what is worth building, how we build it and whether the result creates value for people, planet and business at the same time.

Mindset

How I tend to think, decide and work when there is something worth building.

Think big. Start small. Scale fast.

Set an ambitious direction, start with the smallest useful test and scale only when reality gives us a reason to.

Execution over fear.

Move before everything is certain. Take the next useful step, learn from what happens and adjust without losing momentum.

Learn, unlearn, relearn.

Stay curious, question your own assumptions and be willing to change your mind when better evidence or experience appears.

People, planet & profit.

Build for economic value, human value and wider impact together, rather than treating one as the price of another.

Human-centric by design.

Use technology to strengthen human capability, judgement and agency, while keeping the people affected by it in view.

Different lenses. Same person.

Curious, energetic, structured and direct.

Taken together, my profile points to someone who seeks novelty, engages readily with people, likes structure around goals and is unusually willing to challenge assumptions rather than accommodate them.

The same pattern has a clear edge: directness can become impatience, intensity can rise under pressure, and conviction can make it easy to move faster than other people are ready for.

Express Builder

In BG5, the Express Builder is described as someone who responds first and can then move into action very quickly, often getting things up and running faster than a more step-by-step builder.

That speed comes with a trade-off the framework calls out directly: steps can be skipped and may need to be revisited. In my result, bigger decisions also benefit from waiting for emotional clarity before committing.

ENFP

Myers-Briggs describes ENFPs as enthusiastic, imaginative and quick to connect events and information into patterns. The type is oriented towards possibilities, people, novelty and flexibility.

That maps well to work where the answer is still open and ideas need to be connected quickly. The trade-off is that new possibilities can stay more interesting than narrowing, repeating and finishing.

7w8

The Enneagram Institute describes Type 7 as optimistic, versatile and variety-seeking, with a tendency towards overextension, impatience and impulsiveness. The 8 wing adds a more assertive edge.

What I recognise most is the tension between momentum and depth. Enthusiasm and initiative are useful, but only if I stay with difficult things long enough to finish them and learn from them.

Manifesting Generator

In Human Design, a Manifesting Generator is still a Generator: the strategy is to respond, then move quickly once there is a real yes. Emotional Authority adds the idea that larger decisions need time to reach clarity.

My 2/4 profile adds another theme: natural strengths tend to develop in private but are often called out through relationships and networks. That combination is the part of the framework I recognise most.
