Where I add value

I do my best work in the early stages, when a complex challenge calls for fresh thinking and a clear direction.

I combine creative thinking with strategic judgement and commercial understanding. I think holistically, looking for ways people, planet and profit can reinforce one another.

I can give ambitious ideas direction, bring people together and inspire them to work towards a shared goal. I'm equally capable of going deep into the details and building things myself.

AI is at the heart of everything I do. I'm quick to adapt and willing to change course radically when new perspectives or technologies open up better opportunities.

Waymaking

These are not separate parts of how I work. I think of the way they come together as Waymaking.

Creative thinking

I like opening a problem up before narrowing it down. I question the assumptions behind the obvious answer, connect things that do not normally sit together and look at what new technology, changing needs or a different perspective makes possible.

The aim is not novelty for its own sake. It is to find better options that were easy to miss before.

Strategic judgement

Once the possibilities are on the table, the work changes. Now it is about choosing what is worth pursuing, what matters now, what can wait, and what today's decision makes easier or harder later.

The quickest route to the next milestone can still be the wrong one if it closes off better options afterwards.

Commercial understanding

Then the idea has to meet the real world. Who is it for? What matters enough for someone to change what they do? Why would they choose it? Can the economics work?

I would rather find that out early. Sometimes a gravel road is enough: build the smallest useful version, let people use it, learn, then decide what deserves more.

Building

Building is part of the thinking. A prototype, a product, a workflow or the first version of a business turns assumptions into something people can respond to.

It gives you evidence, exposes what the plan missed and makes the next decision better. That is why I like getting close enough to the work to build the first useful version myself.

There is a question running through all of it: is this worth building?

New is not automatically better. I look at people, planet and profit together: can it stand on sound economics, does it make something better for the people involved, and what does it change around them?

For me, that is what it means to be a Waymaker.

You do not need to know the whole route in advance, and you do not need to build every part yourself. What matters is not how much of the road you build. It is what the road makes possible, and whether others can use it, improve it and take it further.

The shift I see

The next decade will test even the strongest market positions.

AI is changing how companies are built and run. Even market leaders may face unexpected competition from companies a fraction of their size. The challenge is turning AI into a core capability that creates greater value for customers and strengthens the business.

Large organisations can combine their scale and customer trust with startup-like agility. Startups can gain capabilities that once required much larger teams. Both can increase productivity, use capital more efficiently and bring the right ideas to market faster.

But greater capacity doesn't automatically make a company more competitive. AI raises the stakes: it amplifies the value of what a company gets right and the cost of what it gets wrong.

Realising AI's potential means rethinking how companies are organised and led. That takes a customer-centric mindset, a culture that innovates and adapts quickly, and the judgement to prioritise and know when to stop.

The real opportunity isn't AI itself. It's the companies we can build around it.
